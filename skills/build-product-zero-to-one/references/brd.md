# BRD

## Purpose

Turn a raw idea into a concise statement of who needs the product, why it matters, how it differs, and how initial users can be reached.

## Questions and market research

Resolve exactly these four questions:

1. Who are the users, and which people or organizations does the product serve?
2. What pain do they experience, and in what situation do they need the product?
3. What is the value proposition, and how is it different from existing alternatives?
4. How will the product acquire its first users, and is the expected acquisition cost low, medium, or high?

First obtain sufficiently specific user answers to questions 1 and 2. Summarize relevant answers already present in the user's request, ask only what remains missing or ambiguous, and wait when more input is needed.

Before asking or confirming question 3, use `$deep-research` to investigate the market defined by those users and pains. Focus on direct and indirect alternatives, their target users, value propositions and capabilities, evidence of strengths, complaints and unmet needs, and pricing or acquisition only when relevant to differentiation.

Present a concise, objective market landscape without proposing differentiation directions, ranking options, or recommending a choice. Then ask the user to provide or revise question 3 in light of the evidence. Ask question 4 only if the user has not already answered it.

Ask follow-up questions only when an answer is too broad to make an MVP decision. Treat acquisition cost as an estimate and record the reasoning, not invented precision.

## Artifact

After all four questions are clear, write `.atectix/zero-to-one-product-build/01-brd.md` with one section per question. Mark uncertain claims as assumptions. Keep the document concise enough for the user to review in one pass.

## Exit

Summarize the four answers and ask the user to confirm them. Do not introduce pricing, LTV, market sizing, moats, or long-term strategy unless the user requests them.
